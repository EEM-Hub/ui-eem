## "Above the Fold" — Content Placement and Visibility in Print and Web

This page covers the concept of "above the fold," originating in newspaper publishing to describe the upper half of a front page visible when a paper is folded for display. The term has been adopted in web design, advertising, SEO, and even resume writing to describe the most prominent, immediately visible content area.

## Key Concepts

- **Above the fold (print):** The upper half of a newspaper's front page, visible when folded on a newsstand. Editors place the most important or enticing stories here.
- **Above the fold (web):** The portion of a webpage visible without scrolling. Also called "above the scroll."
- **Below the fold:** Content not immediately visible — requires unfolding (print) or scrolling (web). Less prominent by default.
- **No fixed pixel definition:** Screen sizes and responsive layouts mean "the fold" is not a static line on the web.
- **77% non-scroll stat (Nielsen, 2006):** A widely cited finding that 77% of website visitors do not scroll past the initial viewport.
- **84% attention difference (Schade/NNgroup):** Users treat above-the-fold content dramatically differently from below-the-fold content, with a large drop-off in attention.
- **"After the jump":** Slang for content accessible only via clickthrough, analogous to "below the fold."

## Commands and Syntax

No commands or technical syntax. The concept is a design principle, not a configurable setting. Relevant operational details:

- **BlogHer network:** Defined the fold as the top **768 pixels**.
- **FoodieBlogRoll network:** Defined the fold as the top **1000 pixels**.
- Ad networks typically require ad placements above the fold for maximum viewability.

## Relationships

- **Web design and UX:** Directly informs layout decisions — place critical content and CTAs in the initial viewport.
- **Advertising and viewability:** Google research confirms significant viewability drop-off below the fold; ad placement standards depend on this.
- **SEO / search ranking:** High search result placement is analogous to "above the fold" — organic optimization aims to get a site into the visible portion of search results, especially page one.
- **Resume/CV writing:** The "hot zone" (top third of a resume) follows the same principle — the most relevant information must appear first to capture recruiter attention.
- **Responsive design:** Complicates the fold concept since layouts reflow based on browser/device size.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- The term originates from **physical newspaper display** — papers folded on newsstands show only the top half.
- On the web, there is **no universal pixel boundary** for the fold due to varying screen resolutions and responsive design.
- Jakob Nielsen's 2006 study: **77% of visitors do not scroll**.
- NNgroup found an **84% average difference** in user engagement above vs. below the fold.
- Advertisers and ad networks **require above-the-fold placement** for higher viewability; Google research supports this.
- The Apple vs. Samsung case is a notable example: Apple used JavaScript to push a court-ordered acknowledgment **below the fold** on its UK site, drawing legal complaints.
- The concept extends beyond web/print to **any context where initial visibility determines engagement** (resumes, search results).
