## Accessibility: Design for People with Disabilities

This page covers accessibility as the design of products, devices, services, vehicles, and environments to be usable by people with disabilities. It spans legislation, assistive technology, employment, transportation, housing, and public space — emphasizing both direct access (unassisted) and indirect access (via assistive technology).

## Key Concepts

- **Accessibility** is the design of systems so disabled people can use them; it ensures both direct (unassisted) and indirect (assistive-technology-compatible) access.
- **Accessibility vs. usability**: Usability measures how effectively specified users achieve goals; accessibility ensures no barriers exist for people with disabilities.
- **Accessibility vs. universal design**: Universal design creates single solutions for the widest range of people; accessible design specifically eliminates barriers for disabled users.
- **Assistive technology** creates new devices enabling otherwise impossible tasks (e.g., screen readers, hearing aids).
- **Adaptive technology** modifies existing devices or methods for new uses (e.g., remote controls, autocomplete, widened wheelchair tires).
- **Disability management (DM)** is a specialized HR area supporting integration and retention of workers with disabilities.
- **Low-floor vehicles** remove steps from passenger cabins, enabling wheelchair and pushchair access on buses, trams, and trains.
- **Kneeling bus** — a low-floor bus that can temporarily lower itself to ground level for wheelchair access.
- Accessible design benefits everyone, not only disabled users — it helps eliminate the digital divide and knowledge divide.

## Commands and Syntax

Not applicable (this is a policy/concept domain, not a technical configuration topic). However, key standards and frameworks referenced:

- **WCAG** (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) — the standard for web accessibility testing
- **Section 508** (US) — amendment to the Rehabilitation Act requiring accessible federal IT
- **International Symbol of Access** — the blue wheelchair icon denoting accessible areas
- **Audio induction loop** — technology broadcasting announcements directly into hearing aids
- **QR codes** used by South Western Railway for passengers to request accessibility assistance with 10 minutes' notice

## Relationships

- **Universal design** — broader concept; accessibility is a focused subset ensuring no barriers for disabled users
- **Web accessibility** — digital-specific application of accessibility principles (governed by WCAG)
- **Assistive technology** — the tooling layer that enables indirect access (screen readers, hearing aids, wheelchair lifts)
- **Disability rights movement** — the advocacy framework driving accessibility legislation worldwide
- **UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)** — Article 9 commits signatories to full accessibility; referenced across transportation, public space, and voting
- **Employment law** — ADA (US), Equality Act (UK), PWD Act (India) each create workplace accessibility obligations
- **Transport planning** — UK Community Planning Acts require local authorities to produce Accessibility Plans within Local Transport Plans
- **Housing** — connected to visitability movement and occupational therapy for home adaptation

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Accessibility ensures both **direct access** (unassisted) and **indirect access** (assistive technology compatible).
- **Accessibility ≠ usability** — usability is about effectiveness/efficiency for specified users; accessibility is about removing barriers for disabled users.
- **Accessibility ≠ universal design** — universal design targets the widest population with a single solution; accessibility specifically eliminates disability barriers.
- **Assistive technology** = new devices; **adaptive technology** = modifications to existing devices.
- Key legislation by country: **ADA 1990** (US), **DDA 1992** (Australia), **Equality Act 2010** (UK), **Accessible Canada Act 2019** (Canada).
- **Section 508** governs US federal IT accessibility; **WCAG** is the web accessibility standard.
- **European Accessibility Act (EAA)** enforcement begins **June 2025**.
- UN CRPD **Article 9** — full accessibility commitment for signatory nations.
- **Low-floor vehicles** were the key 1990s transport accessibility innovation; **kneeling buses** lower temporarily to ground level.
- UK requires **Accessibility Plans** under Community Planning legislation, incorporated into **Local Transport Plans**.
- Employment gap: US workers with disabilities had **12.9% unemployment** vs. **7.3%** for non-disabled (2012 data); ~25% earnings gap.
- In India, disability employment in the private sector was **0.28%**, and **0.05%** in multinationals (1999 survey).
- Disability management is an HR specialization; a key gap is managing employees who **acquire** disabilities during employment (not just pre-existing conditions).
