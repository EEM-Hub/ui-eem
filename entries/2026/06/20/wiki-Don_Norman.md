## Don Norman: Pioneer of User-Centered Design and Cognitive Engineering

Donald Arthur Norman (b. 1935) is an American researcher, professor, and author who fundamentally shaped the fields of user-centered design, usability engineering, and cognitive science. He is best known for *The Design of Everyday Things*, co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group, and is credited with first using "User Experience" in a job title while at Apple.

## Key Concepts

- **User-centered design (UCD)**: Term Norman introduced in 1986; design based on user needs rather than secondary considerations like aesthetics. Involves simplifying task structure, making things visible, correct mapping, exploiting constraints, designing for error, and leveraging affordances.
- **Human-centered design (HCD)**: Norman's revised preferred term (replacing UCD in the 2013 edition of *Design of Everyday Things*); "an approach that puts human needs, capabilities, and behavior first, then designs to accommodate those needs, capabilities, and ways of behaving."
- **Cognitive artifacts**: Artificial devices that maintain, display, or operate upon information to serve a representational function and affect human cognitive performance.
- **Affordances**: Properties of objects that suggest how they can be used; central to Norman's design philosophy.
- **Seven stages of action**: Norman's model describing how users interact with systems (gulf of execution and gulf of evaluation).
- **Norman-Shallice model**: Framework of attentional control of executive functioning, including the supervisory attentional system (developed with Tim Shallice).
- **JND (just-noticeable difference)**: The name of Norman's website (jnd.org), referencing the psychophysics concept and symbolizing his goal to make a meaningful difference.
- **Emotional design**: Norman's later acknowledgment that aesthetics, pleasure, and emotion play critically important roles in design, not just usability.

Not applicable — this is a biographical/theoretical topic rather than a technical procedures page. However, Norman's design principles function as a practitioner checklist:

1. Make things visible (visibility)
2. Provide good mappings (relationship between controls and outcomes)
3. Use constraints to guide correct use
4. Design for error (assume users will make mistakes)
5. Simplify task structures
6. Use affordances and signifiers
7. Apply the seven stages of action as an evaluation framework

## Relationships

- **Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g)**: Co-founded in 1998 with Jakob Nielsen; a consultancy focused on evidence-based UX.
- **Cognitive science**: Norman was founding chair of the Dept. of Cognitive Science at UCSD and co-organized the Cognitive Science Society (first meeting 1979).
- **Cognitive systems engineering**: Norman shaped this field, bridging cognitive science with engineering practice.
- **Apple Computer**: Served as Apple Fellow and VP of the Advanced Technology Group (1993–); first to use "User Experience Architect" as a job title.
- **Three Mile Island**: Was part of the investigation team for the 1979 nuclear accident, a formative case study in human factors and interface design failures.
- **HCI (Human-Computer Interaction)**: Norman's 1981 article criticizing Unix's user interface ("The truth about Unix: The user interface is horrid") established him as a leading voice in usability.
- **IDEO**: Norman is a fellow of this design firm.
- **Design Lab at UCSD**: Norman is the founding director (established 2014).

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Norman coined "user-centered design" in 1986 (in *User Centered System Design*, co-edited with Stephen Draper).
- He later replaced "user-centered design" with "human-centered design" in the revised 2013 edition of *The Design of Everyday Things*, acknowledging the importance of aesthetics and emotion.
- The original title of *The Design of Everyday Things* was *The Psychology of Everyday Things* (1988).
- Norman was the first person to hold the job title "User Experience Architect" (at Apple, 1993).
- The Nielsen Norman Group was founded in 1998.
- Norman's educational background spans electrical engineering (BS from MIT, MS from UPenn) and psychology (PhD from UPenn under R. Duncan Luce).
- Key awards: ACM Fellow (2001), Benjamin Franklin Medal in Computer and Cognitive Science (2006), National Academy of Engineering member (2011).
- The supervisory attentional system is part of the Norman-Shallice model of executive attention.
- Norman controversially argued that design researchers have had little impact on product innovation — breakthroughs come from technologists, while academics refine existing products.
- *Emotional Design* (2004) was Norman's correction to his earlier dismissal of aesthetics in design.
