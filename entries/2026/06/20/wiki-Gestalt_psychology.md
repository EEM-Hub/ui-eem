## Gestalt Psychology: Principles of Perceptual Organization

Gestalt psychology is a school of psychology and theory of perception that emerged in early 20th-century Germany and Austria. It emphasizes that humans perceive entire patterns and configurations rather than individual components, rejecting the elementalist and structuralist approaches of Wundt and Titchener. The German word "Gestalt" means "form," "pattern," or "configuration."

## Key Concepts

- **Core thesis**: The whole is *something else* than the sum of its parts — not merely "more than" the sum. Summing parts is a meaningless procedure; the whole-part relationship is what carries meaning (Koffka's formulation).
- **Founded by**: Max Wertheimer, Kurt Koffka, and Wolfgang Köhler in the early 20th century.
- **Rejected structuralism**, which rested on three pillars: atomism (knowledge built from elementary constituents), sensationalism (atoms of thought are sense impressions), and associationism (complex ideas arise from association of simpler ones).
- **Principle of totality**: Conscious experience must be considered globally; each component is part of a system of dynamic relationships (holism).
- **Psychophysical isomorphism**: Hypothesizes a correlation between conscious experience and cerebral activity.
- **Phi phenomenon**: Wertheimer's 1912 discovery of perceived motion from alternating stationary lights — pure phenomenal motion with no moving object. Marks the formal beginning of Gestalt psychology.
- **Gestalt-qualität** ("form-quality"): Concept introduced by Christian von Ehrenfels (1890) before Gestalt psychology proper — a melody retains identity when transposed to a new key because the form-quality transcends its component notes.
- **Four key perceptual properties**: Emergence, reification, multistability, and invariance.
- **Gestalt therapy is distinct** from Gestalt psychology — Fritz Perls borrowed terminology but the two have no substantive scientific relationship (per Mary Henle's critique).

## Commands and Syntax

Not applicable (theoretical/conceptual domain). However, the core "laws" function as operational rules for predicting perceptual grouping:

- **Law of Prägnanz** (good Gestalt): People perceive things as regular, orderly, symmetrical, and simple. This is the overarching principle; all other laws are refinements.
- **Law of Proximity**: Elements close together are grouped together.
- **Law of Similarity**: Elements similar in shape, color, or size are grouped together.
- **Law of Closure**: The mind completes incomplete figures into whole shapes (e.g., IBM logo's horizontal bars perceived as letters).
- **Law of Symmetry**: The mind perceives objects as forming around a center point; symmetrical elements are perceptually connected.
- **Law of Common Fate**: Elements moving in the same direction are grouped together.
- **Law of Continuity** (good continuation): Elements aligned within an object are grouped; intersecting objects are perceived as uninterrupted entities.
- **Law of Past Experience**: Visual stimuli may be categorized according to prior experience.

## Relationships

- **Opposes structuralism** (Helmholtz, Wundt, Titchener) and its empiricist roots in atomism, sensationalism, and associationism.
- **Precursors**: Christian von Ehrenfels (Gestalt-qualität), School of Brentano, Ernst Mach (analysis of sensations), David Hume, Kant, Goethe.
- **Connects to computational vision**: David Marr's theories provide alternative explanations for invariance and object classification.
- **Influenced but distinct from Gestalt therapy**: Fritz and Laura Perls adapted some ideas via Kurt Goldstein's neurological applications, but the scientific relationship is minimal.
- **Gestalt theoretical psychotherapy** is the form of psychotherapy actually consistently based on Gestalt psychology (unlike Gestalt therapy).
- **Connects to**: motion perception, form/contour perception, perceptual constancy, illusory contours, multistable perception (Necker cube, Rubin vase).
- **Köhler's insight learning** in chimpanzees challenged associationist learning theories of Pavlov and Thorndike.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- The correct formulation is "the whole is *something else* than the sum of its parts" — not "more than" (Koffka).
- **Founders**: Wertheimer, Koffka, Köhler — know all three and their distinct contributions (Wertheimer: phi phenomenon; Köhler: insight learning in chimps, studied under Max Planck; Koffka: *Principles of Gestalt Psychology*, introduced Gestalt to American audience).
- **1912**: Wertheimer's phi phenomenon publication marks the formal beginning of Gestalt psychology.
- **Prägnanz** is the fundamental/overarching law; all other grouping laws are refinements of it.
- **Reification**: Perception contains more spatial information than the stimulus (e.g., seeing a triangle in Kanizsa figures where none is drawn).
- **Multistability**: Ambiguous figures alternate between interpretations (Necker cube, Rubin vase).
- **Invariance**: Objects recognized regardless of rotation, translation, scale, elastic deformation, or different graphic elements.
- Gestalt psychology is **not** Gestalt therapy — this is a frequently tested distinction.
- Von Ehrenfels introduced the concept of Gestalt in **1890**, before the school formally began.
- All core Gestalt members emigrated to the US by **1935** due to Nazi persecution.
