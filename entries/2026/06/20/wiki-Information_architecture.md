## Information Architecture: Structural Design of Shared Information Environments

Information architecture (IA) is the structural design of shared information environments — particularly the organization of websites, intranets, and software — to support usability and findability. Coined by Richard Saul Wurman, IA has grown into a community of practice that applies principles from design, architecture, and information science to digital spaces. The term carries different meanings across disciplines including UX design, information systems, data architecture, and systems design.

## Key Concepts

- **Core definition**: The combination of organization, labeling, search, and navigation systems within websites and intranets
- **Four context-dependent definitions**:
  - **UX**: Organizing and labelling digital environments to support findability and usability
  - **Information systems**: Navigational aid to the content of information-rich systems
  - **Data architecture**: A subset of data architecture focused on arranging usable data for end users
  - **Systems design**: A component of enterprise architecture dealing with the information layer
- **Eight principles of IA** (Dan Brown, 2010): objects, choices, disclosure, exemplars, front doors, multiple classification, focused navigation, growth
- **Findability and usability** are the two primary goals IA serves
- IA applies to websites, intranets, online communities, and software applications

## Commands and Syntax

No CLI commands or configuration syntax — IA is a design discipline. Key methods and artifacts include:

- **Card sorting** — elicitation technique for discovering user mental models of content categories
- **Tree testing** — evaluating topic hierarchies for findability before building navigation
- **Site maps** — visual or structured representations of page hierarchy
- **Controlled vocabularies** — standardized term sets for consistent labeling
- **Faceted classification** — multi-dimensional categorization allowing multiple navigation paths to the same content

## Relationships

- **Parent discipline**: Information science (organization, retrieval, behavior, management)
- **Sibling to**: Interaction design, content strategy, user experience design
- **Subset of**: Data architecture (in one interpretation)
- **Component of**: Enterprise architecture (in systems design contexts)
- **Feeds into**: Semantic Web (IA listed as a related topic to semantic standards like RDF, OWL, SKOS)
- **Related practices**: Content management, data management, knowledge visualization, wayfinding
- **Key foundational text**: *Information Architecture for the World Wide Web* by Morville & Rosenfeld (3rd ed., 2007)

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Richard Saul Wurman coined the term "information architecture"
- Peter Morville and Louis Rosenfeld co-authored the canonical reference text (1998–2015 editions)
- IA comprises four systems: **organization, labeling, search, and navigation**
- The field sits at the intersection of **design, architecture, and information science**
- IA has different scopes depending on discipline — UX practitioners include user experience factors; some systems design practitioners restrict IA strictly to classification and information retrieval
- The eight named principles (objects, choices, disclosure, exemplars, front doors, multiple classification, focused navigation, growth) are a testable enumeration
- IA is distinct from but related to interaction design (behavior) and content strategy (substance and governance)
