## Interaction Design (IxD): Foundations, Methodologies, and Dimensions

Interaction design (IxD) is the practice of designing interactive digital products, environments, systems, and services, with a primary focus on **behavior** rather than form. Unlike science or engineering, IxD is a synthesis-oriented design field—it imagines how things *could be* rather than analyzing how they are. It draws on psychology, human-computer interaction, information architecture, and user research to create designs tailored to user needs within technical and business constraints.

## Key Concepts

- **IxD focuses on behavior**, not just form—distinguishing it from other design disciplines
- **Cognitive friction**: when an interface is complex, inconsistent, and behaves unexpectedly across different modes (term from Alan Cooper)
- **Personas**: archetypes describing user goals and observed behavior patterns; they encapsulate behavioral data in a way designers and stakeholders can understand and relate to
- **Cognitive dimensions**: a lightweight vocabulary for evaluating design quality (e.g., consistency, error-proneness, viscosity, premature commitment, hard mental operations)
- **Design maneuvers**: modifications to a design along a particular cognitive dimension
- **Goal-directed design**: methodology focused on satisfying user needs and desires, not provider needs
- **Usability**: the quality attribute describing how usable an interface is (Jakob Nielsen's definition)
- **Affective interaction design**: designing for emotional responses—products should convey positive emotions and avoid negative ones; affective aspects influence perceived usability
- **Five dimensions of IxD**: Words (1D), Visual representations (2D), Physical objects/space (3D), Time (4D), Behavior (5D—added by Kevin Silver)
- IxD practitioners commonly hold titles of **UX designer** or **UI designer**
- IxD tools like **Figma** and **Adobe XD** help manage the inherent complexity of designing for many users, contexts, and system states

## Commands and Syntax

Not applicable—this is a conceptual/theoretical domain. However, key frameworks and heuristics to know:

- **Shneiderman's Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design**: well-known heuristics for creating usable systems
- **Don Norman's Emotional Design Model**: three levels of emotional response (visceral, behavioral, reflective)
- **Patrick Jordan's Pleasure Model**: four types of pleasure in product interaction
- **McCarthy & Wright's Technology as Experience Framework**: understanding technology through experience

## Relationships

- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)**: academic research discipline providing methods for describing and testing interface usability; IxD is a practice-oriented counterpart
- **Industrial design**: overlapping core principles (form, color, aesthetics, human perception, usability); IxD extends these to digital behavior
- **Cognitive psychology**: provides foundational concepts—mental models, mapping, interface metaphors, affordances (Donald Norman's *The Design of Everyday Things*)
- **Human factors/ergonomics**: anthropometry, biomechanics, kinesiology, physiology, and psychology ground IxD in physical and cognitive human capabilities
- **Information architecture**: closely allied discipline for structuring content and navigation
- **User interface design**: IxD overlaps but emphasizes behavior over time and system response to user experience
- **Architecture**: increasingly relevant as IxD addresses ubiquitous computing, urban informatics, and spatial/contextual design
- **User experience design (UXD)**: broader umbrella; IxD is a core component focused specifically on interactive behavior

## Exam-Relevant Points

- **Term coined by Bill Moggridge and Bill Verplank** in the mid-1980s; Moggridge's earlier term was "soft-face" (1984)
- **First academic program**: Carnegie Mellon University, 1994—Master of Design in Interaction Design
- **Earliest related programs**: Visible Language Workshop (Muriel Cooper, MIT, 1975) and Interactive Telecommunications Program (NYU, 1979, founded by Martin Elton, later led by Red Burns)
- **Key institutions**: Royal College of Art (Gillian Crampton Smith, 1990), Interaction Design Institute Ivrea (2001, closed 2007), Copenhagen Institute of Interaction Design (2007), The Interactive Institute (Sweden, 1998)
- **Interaction Design Association (IxDA)**: founded 2003, 80,000+ members, 173+ local groups; hosts annual Interaction conference; Interaction Awards ended August 2024
- **Five dimensions**: Words, Visual representations, Physical objects/space, Time, Behavior—first four from Crampton Smith, fifth added by Kevin Silver
- **Key texts**: Cooper's *About Face 3* and *The Inmates Are Running the Asylum*; Moggridge's *Designing Interactions*; Norman's *The Design of Everyday Things*; Goodwin's *Designing for the Digital Age*
- IxD is distinguished from software engineering by its focus on **user needs and experience** rather than technical stakeholder needs
- Affective design elements (fonts, color palettes, animations, sound, dynamic icons) demonstrably **affect perceptions of usability**
