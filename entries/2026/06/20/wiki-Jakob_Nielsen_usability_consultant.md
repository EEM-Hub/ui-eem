## Jakob Nielsen's Usability Principles and Heuristic Evaluation

Jakob Nielsen (b. 1957) is a Danish HCI researcher, web usability consultant, and co-founder of Nielsen Norman Group. He is widely regarded as the most influential figure in web usability, having pioneered heuristic evaluation, the "5 users" testing principle, and a set of ten usability heuristics that remain the most widely used UI design framework. His career spans IBM, Bellcore, Sun Microsystems, and his own consultancies.

## Key Concepts

- **Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics** (finalized 2005): Visibility of system status; Match between system and real world; User control and freedom; Consistency and standards; Error prevention; Recognition rather than recall; Flexibility and efficiency of use; Aesthetic and minimalist design; Help users recognize/diagnose/recover from errors; Help and documentation.
- **Jakob's Law**: Users form expectations for a site based on mental models from other sites. Minimize design changes to preserve ease of use.
- **5-User Testing Principle**: Five test users per usability test session is sufficient to uncover most usability problems. Enables frequent, low-cost testing at multiple development stages rather than one expensive test.
- **Five Usability Goals** (from *Usability Engineering*, 1993): Learnability, Efficiency, Memorability, low Error rate, Satisfaction.
- **Heuristic Evaluation**: A usability inspection method Nielsen invented where evaluators judge a UI against established heuristics rather than running full user tests. Originally published with Rolf Molich (1989-1990), refined through 1994 and 2005.
- **Discount Usability**: Nielsen's philosophy that elaborate usability tests waste resources; iterative, lightweight evaluation finds problems faster and cheaper.

## Commands and Syntax

Not applicable — this is a biographical/conceptual topic, not a technical tool. However, the heuristic evaluation *procedure* involves:

1. Select 3-5 evaluators (not end users — UX experts).
2. Each evaluator independently inspects the interface against the 10 heuristics.
3. Aggregate findings and rank by severity.
4. Iterate on design fixes and re-evaluate.

## Relationships

- **Heuristic Evaluation** connects to broader **usability inspection methods** (cognitive walkthrough, formal inspection).
- **Nielsen Norman Group** co-founded with **Donald Norman**, who coined "user experience" — bridges Nielsen's usability engineering with Norman's cognitive/emotional design perspective.
- **Jakob's Law** is one of the **Laws of UX** (a curated set by Jon Yablonski alongside Fitts's Law, Hick's Law, etc.).
- **Responsive web design** debate: Nielsen's 2012 recommendation for separate mobile sites conflicted with the responsive design movement (Ethan Marcotte's approach), highlighting the tension between usability-optimal and implementation-practical strategies.
- **Mental models** (cognitive psychology) underpin Jakob's Law and several of the 10 heuristics (match between system and real world, recognition vs. recall).

## Exam-Relevant Points

- **Memorize all 10 heuristics by name** — this is the single most tested usability framework in HCI/UX certification exams.
- **5 users is the magic number** for usability testing — based on Nielsen & Landauer's 1993 mathematical model showing diminishing returns beyond 5 participants per test round.
- **Heuristic evaluation is an expert review method**, not a user testing method — a common exam distinction.
- **The five usability goals** (Learnability, Efficiency, Memorability, Errors, Satisfaction) define measurable quality components of usability.
- **Jakob's Law** emphasizes designing with conventions users already know — users spend most of their time on *other* sites.
- **Key criticism**: No research-based validation of the 10 heuristics exists; they were developed for simpler 1990s interfaces and may oversimplify modern UI complexity.
- **Nielsen co-founded NNG in 1998** with Donald Norman — know both founders and the company's focus on human-centered design.
- **Alertbox** was his influential fortnightly column on useit.com, started 1996 — historically significant as one of the first regular web usability publications.
