## Mental Models: Internal Representations of External Reality

This page covers the concept of **mental models** — internal cognitive representations that people construct to understand, reason about, and predict events in the external world. It traces the concept's history from Kenneth Craik's 1943 coinage through its development in cognitive science, reasoning theory, human-computer interaction, system dynamics, and organizational learning.

## Key Concepts

- **Mental model**: An internal, simplified representation of external reality used to anticipate events, shape behavior, solve problems, and perform tasks.
- **Coined by Kenneth Craik (1943)** in *The Nature of Explanation*; the mind constructs "small-scale models" of reality.
- **Jay Wright Forrester's definition**: The image of the world we carry in our heads is just a model — selected concepts and relationships representing the real system.
- Mental models are **iconic**: each part of the model corresponds to a part of what it represents.
- Based on a **principle of truth**: they typically represent only what is true in a given possibility, though they can temporarily represent what is false (counterfactual reasoning).
- Each mental model represents **one possibility**, capturing what is common to all ways that possibility may occur.
- Mental models are **flexible** (variable in both positive and negative ways), **limited** by working memory constraints, and act as **information filters** causing selective perception.
- People are often **hardly conscious** of their own mental models.
- Closely related to **schemas** (psychology) and **conceptual models**, but distinct from formal logical rules of inference.
- **Philip Johnson-Laird and Ruth Byrne** developed the mental model theory of reasoning: reasoning depends on mental models, not on logical form.
- Validity is tested by checking if a conclusion holds across **all models of the premises**; a counterexample refutes an invalid inference.

## Commands and Syntax

No technical commands apply. However, three **graphical methods** for expressing mental models of dynamic systems are described:

- **Causal loop diagrams** — show direction of information connections, causality, and feedback loops.
- **System structure diagrams** — express the structure of qualitative dynamic systems.
- **Stock and flow diagrams** — quantify the structure of a dynamic system.

Tools like **Mental Modeler** (fuzzy-logic cognitive mapping) have been developed for participatory modeling, collecting/comparing individual mental models.

## Relationships

- **Schema (psychology)** and **conceptual models** are cognitively adjacent concepts.
- **System dynamics** extends mental models into explicit, sharable, comparable models.
- **Systemic thinking** seeks to improve mental models to improve dynamic decision quality.
- **Human-computer interaction / usability**: Donald Norman and Steve Krug applied mental models to UI design (users' models of how interfaces work).
- **Discourse comprehension**: Kintsch and van Dijk's "situation model" connects mental models to language understanding.
- **Picture theory of language** (Wittgenstein) — mental models share the structural-analogy property.
- **Counterfactual thinking** — mental models can represent false states temporarily assumed true.
- **Organizational learning** — mental models are fundamental; single-loop learning changes decisions within fixed models, while **double-loop learning** changes the models themselves.
- Competing theories of reasoning: **formal rules of inference**, **domain-specific rules**, and **probabilistic/Bayesian** approaches challenge the mental model account.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Kenneth Craik coined "mental model" in **1943**; Johnson-Laird's major publication was **1983**.
- Mental models are **not** the same as formal logical rules — they are structural analogues of the situations they represent.
- The **counterexample** mechanism: reasoners validate conclusions by searching for a possibility where premises hold but the conclusion does not.
- **Single-loop learning**: decisions change but mental models remain the same. **Double-loop learning**: the mental models themselves are revised.
- Mental models are constrained by **working memory** limits.
- Mental models cause **selective perception** — people perceive only information that fits their model.
- Three methods for expressing dynamic system mental models: causal loop diagrams, system structure diagrams, stock and flow diagrams.
- Neuroscience evidence: humans possess an internal mental model of **gravity's effects on object motion**, demonstrated in weightlessness experiments and neuroimaging studies.
- Charlie Munger popularized **multi-disciplinary mental models** for business and investment decision-making.
