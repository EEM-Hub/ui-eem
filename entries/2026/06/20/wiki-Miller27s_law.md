## Miller's Law (Disambiguation of Four Principles)

This page disambiguates four unrelated principles that all share the name "Miller's law," spanning communication, linguistics, psychology, and software development.

## Key Concepts

- **Miller's Law (Communication)**: Formulated by George Armitage Miller (1920–2012), Princeton psychologist. Principle: suspend judgment about what someone is saying; assume they are being truthful and imagine what could be true about it, before applying personal interpretation.
- **Miller's Law (Linguistics)**: A sound law describing postnasal deaspiration in ancient Greek. Unrelated to the other three.
- **Miller's Law (Psychology)**: Also by George A. Miller. The observation that working memory capacity is approximately **7 ± 2** objects. Published in 1956 in *Psychological Review* as "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two."
- **Miller's Law (Software Development)**: Formulated by Mike Beltzner, named after Dave Miller (Bugzilla product owner). States that all discussions of incremental updates to Bugzilla will inevitably trend toward proposals for large-scale redesigns or replacements.

## Relationships

- **Principle of Charity** — the communication version of Miller's Law is closely related to the philosophical principle of charity (interpreting others' statements in the most rational/favorable way).
- **Suspension of Judgment** — the communication law explicitly requires suspending judgment as a prerequisite for understanding.
- **Cognitive Load / UX Design** — the psychology version (7 ± 2) is foundational in UI/UX design, information architecture, and instructional design for chunking information.
- **Scope Creep** — the software development version describes a pattern closely related to scope creep and second-system effect.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- The **communication** and **psychology** versions are both by George A. Miller — do not confuse them.
- The psychology version's magic number is **7 ± 2** (not exactly 7), published in **1956**.
- The communication version requires you to **assume truthfulness first**, then reason about what could make the statement true — it is not about agreeing, but about understanding.
- The software development version is named after **Dave Miller** (Bugzilla), not George Miller.
- All four principles are **unrelated** despite sharing the same name.
