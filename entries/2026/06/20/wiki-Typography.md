## Typography: The Art and Technique of Arranging Type

Typography is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and visually appealing. It encompasses the selection of typefaces, point sizes, line lengths, line spacing, letter spacing, and kerning. The field spans from traditional metal movable type through to modern digital type design, and is practiced by graphic designers, art directors, and increasingly by anyone who publishes or displays text.

## Key Concepts

- **Typography** involves both the *process* of arranging type and the *style/appearance* of the resulting output — two distinct meanings.
- **Type design** is a related but separate craft; most typographers don't design typefaces, and some type designers don't consider themselves typographers.
- **Legibility** vs. **Readability**: Legibility refers to how easily individual characters are distinguished from one another ("perception"); readability refers to how easy it is to comprehend the text as a whole ("comprehension"). These are separate but related concepts.
- **Type color**: The overall density of ink on the page, determined by typeface, word spacing, leading, and margin depth.
- **Bouma** (word shape): Some typographers argue that overall word shape is essential for readability, contrasting with the parallel letter recognition theory.
- **Saccadic rhythm**: The eye takes in roughly three words per saccadic jump; lines requiring more than 3–4 jumps introduce strain and reading errors.
- **All-caps text** reduces legibility by making words indistinguishable as groups, presenting a uniform line to the eye.
- **Serif vs. sans-serif**: Serif typefaces are traditionally used for body text (prose); sans-serif fonts are often considered to have lower readability for long passages but are used for headings and short text.
- **Old style vs. modern Roman typefaces**: Old style has similarly weighted lines; modern is distinguished by contrast between light and heavy lines.
- **Experimental typography**: Unconventional, artistic approach to type that emphasizes emotion over legibility (associated with Dada, David Carson's *Ray Gun* magazine).

## Commands and Syntax

No commands per se, but key typographic parameters and techniques include:

- **Typeface selection** — chosen based on content genre, historical period, and intended tone
- **Point size** — determines type size; affects legibility
- **Leading** (line spacing) — generous vertical space improves readability
- **Tracking/letter-spacing** — too tight or too loose reduces readability
- **Kerning** — adjusting space between specific pairs of letters
- **X-height and vertical proportions** — influence perceived size and readability
- **Weight and contrast** — varying stroke thickness within and between typefaces
- **Justification** — justified text must be carefully adjusted to prevent readability loss; ragged right is an alternative
- **Style guide formulation** — standardizes typeface usage across a publication (typefaces, case, sizes, italic, bold, color)

## Relationships

- **Type design** is closely related but distinct from typography; type designers create the letterforms that typographers arrange.
- **Calligraphy and hand-lettering** are historical predecessors; early printed typefaces (blackletter/Gothic) imitated scribal hand-lettering styles.
- **Graphic design** and **art direction** rely heavily on typography as a core discipline.
- **Orthography and linguistics** modulate typesetting conventions (e.g., French non-breaking space before colons).
- **Printing technology** — typography evolved alongside movable type (Bi Sheng, Gutenberg), phototypesetting, and digital design tools (Fontographer, personal computers).
- **Readability research** connects to cognitive science (eye movement, saccadic rhythm, visual perception models).
- **Industrial design** — typography appears on appliances, vehicle dashboards, signage, and wearables.
- **Web and digital design** — digitization expanded typographic applications to screens, LCDs, and mobile devices.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- **Bi Sheng** (990–1051) invented movable type during the **Song dynasty** in China (11th century) using ceramic materials — predates Gutenberg.
- **Johannes Gutenberg** (1439) is credited with modern lead-based movable type and the mechanical printing press; the **Gutenberg Bible** was the first book printed this way.
- **Metal movable type** was first invented in **Korea** during the **Goryeo dynasty**, c. 1230.
- **Claude Garamond** was partially responsible for the adoption of **Roman typeface** over Gothic/blackletter during the Renaissance in France.
- **Legibility = perception** (can you distinguish characters?); **Readability = comprehension** (can you understand the text?). Know this distinction.
- **Type color** is not about hue — it refers to overall ink density on the page.
- The "democratization of type" occurred in the mid-1980s when personal computers and software like **Fontographer** made typeface design widely accessible.
- **Roman typeface** traces its development from Greek lapidary letters → Roman monumental capitals → serif typefaces.
- **International Typographic Style** relates to 1920s–1930s international graphics; **Swiss style** emerged in the 1950s–1960s.
- Key typography variables: typeface, point size, line length, line spacing (leading), letter spacing (tracking), and kerning.
