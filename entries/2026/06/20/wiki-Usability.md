## Usability: Principles, Standards, and Evaluation Methods

Usability is the capacity of a system to enable users to perform tasks safely, effectively, and efficiently while enjoying the experience. This page covers the formal definition of usability (including ISO standards), its five measurable components, design principles for achieving usability, and a comprehensive taxonomy of evaluation methods ranging from cognitive modeling to user testing.

## Key Concepts

- **ISO Definition**: "The extent to which a product can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction in a specified context of use."
- **Five components of usability** (Nielsen/Shneiderman framework): Learnability, Efficiency, Memorability, Errors, Satisfaction.
- **Usability vs. Utility**: Usability is how easy/pleasant the interface is; utility is whether it provides needed functionality. Together they form "usefulness."
- **Usability is a non-functional requirement** — it cannot be measured directly, only through indirect measures (e.g., number of reported ease-of-use problems).
- **"Intuitive" interaction** is actually based on familiarity with prior similar systems, not innate understanding. Jef Raskin argued "familiar" is more accurate than "intuitive."
- **Prototypicality**: Users prefer designs that conform to recognized norms and conventions.
- **Usability testing vs. usability engineering**: Testing measures ease of use; engineering is the research and design process that ensures good usability.

## Commands and Syntax

No CLI commands. The key procedural frameworks are:

- **Three design principles** (Gould & Lewis):
  1. Early focus on end users and their tasks
  2. Empirical measurement (quantitative and qualitative)
  3. Iterative design (prototype, test, analyze, refine cycle)

- **GOMS analysis structure**: Goals → Operators → Methods → Selection rules

- **Nielsen's 10 Heuristics** (usability inspection checklist):
  1. Visibility of system status
  2. Match between system and real world
  3. User control and freedom (undo/redo)
  4. Consistency and standards
  5. Error prevention
  6. Recognition rather than recall
  7. Flexibility and efficiency of use
  8. Aesthetic and minimalist design
  9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors
  10. Help and documentation

- **Human Processor Model reference values**: Perceptual processor ~100ms, Cognitive processor ~70ms, Motor processor ~70ms, Working memory capacity ~2 items.

## Relationships

- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)**: Usability is a core concern within HCI and computer science.
- **Ergonomics / Human Factors**: Debated relationship — some view usability as the software specialization of ergonomics; others see ergonomics as physiological and usability as psychological.
- **User-Centered Design / Participatory Design**: Design paradigms that operationalize usability principles by involving users throughout the process.
- **ISO Standards**: ISO 9241 (ergonomics of human-system interaction), ISO/TR 16982:2002 (usability methods), IEC 62366 (medical device usability).
- **Web Usability**: A specialized subfield; Jakob Nielsen's research shows users have low tolerance for difficult designs or slow sites.
- **Accessibility**: Related but distinct — "user friendly" sometimes conflates the two.
- **Evaluation methods form a taxonomy**: Cognitive modeling (GOMS, KLM, parallel design), Inspection methods (heuristic evaluation, card sorting, tree testing, ethnography), and Inquiry methods (task analysis, focus groups).

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Know the **five usability components**: Learnability, Efficiency, Memorability, Errors, Satisfaction — and be able to distinguish them.
- **Nielsen's 10 Heuristics** are the most commonly used industry framework for heuristic evaluation — memorize all ten.
- **Usability is a non-functional requirement** that must be measured indirectly.
- **Three design principles** (early user focus, empirical measurement, iterative design) are foundational to usability engineering.
- **GOMS** = Goals, Operators, Methods, Selection rules — a cognitive modeling technique for analyzing user complexity.
- **Card sorting** helps build information architecture; **tree testing** evaluates it.
- **Heuristic evaluation** is the most popular inspection method due to being quick, cheap, and easy.
- **ISO 9241** is the key standard for ergonomics of human-system interaction (originally for VDTs, now broader).
- **IEC 62366** is specific to medical device usability engineering.
- Distinguish between **usability testing** (measurement) and **usability engineering** (the full design process).
- **"Intuitive" ≈ "familiar"** — interfaces feel intuitive when users transfer knowledge from prior experience, not from innate understanding.
