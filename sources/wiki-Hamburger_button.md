User interface element [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Hamburger_icon.svg/250px-Hamburger_icon.svg.png)](./File:Hamburger_icon.svg)Collapsed menu ("hamburger") icon 

The **hamburger button** is a [button](./Button_(computing)), typically placed in a top corner of a [graphical user interface](./Graphical_user_interface).[[1]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-W3s-howto-1)  Its function is to toggle a [menu](./Menu_(computing)) (sometimes referred to as a **hamburger menu**) or [navigation bar](./Navigation_bar) between being collapsed behind the button or displayed on the screen.

 

The [icon](./Icon_(computing)) which is associated with this button consists of three parallel horizontal bars (≡), known as the **hamburger icon** (sometimes called **collapsed menu icon**). It is intended to resemble the lines of text in a small printed [menu](./Menu).[[2]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Gizmodo-2)[[3]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-WSJ_20130318-3) It is named after its unintentional resemblance to a [hamburger](./Hamburger).

 

## History

 [![Xerox Star computer interface](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Xerox_Star_8010_ViewPoint.jpg/250px-Xerox_Star_8010_ViewPoint.jpg)](./File:Xerox_Star_8010_ViewPoint.jpg)[![Microsoft Windows 1.0 screenshot](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Microsoft_Windows_1.0_screenshot.png/250px-Microsoft_Windows_1.0_screenshot.png)](./File:Microsoft_Windows_1.0_screenshot.png)Early versions of the hamburger button can be seen in the 1980s graphical user interfaces of the [Xerox Star](./Xerox_Star) computer and Microsoft [Windows 1.0](./Windows_1.0) 

The icon was originally designed by [Norm Cox](./Norm_Cox_(designer)) as part of the user interface for the [Xerox Star](./Xerox_Star) personal computer, introduced in 1981.[[4]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Evernote-origin-4) Cox described the icon's creation, saying:[[5]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-5)

 

Its graphic design was meant to be very 'road sign' simple, functionally memorable, and mimic the look of the resulting displayed menu list. With so few pixels to work with, it had to be very distinct, yet simple. I think we only had 16×16 pixels to render the image. (or possibly 13×13...).

 

In possibly its first use after the Xerox Star, the release of [Windows 1.0](./Windows_1.0) in 1985 contained a hamburger icon in each window's control menu.[[6]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-6) It was short-lived, however, as the hamburger icon disappeared in [Windows 2.0](./Windows_2.0) in favor of a single horizontal line denoting the control menu. [Windows 95](./Windows_95) replaced the single line with the program's icon,[[7]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-7) and the hamburger would not return to Windows until a placement on the [start menu](./Start_menu) of the [one-year update](./Windows_10_version_history#Version_1607_(Anniversary_Update)) of [Windows 10](./Windows_10).[[8]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-8)

 

Cox's hamburger icon saw a resurgence starting in 2009 stemming from the limited screen area available to [mobile apps](./Mobile_app).[[2]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Gizmodo-2)[[9]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-PlaceItHist-9)[[10]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Stuff-10)

 

## Variants

 [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Editing_Wikipedia_mobile_screenshot_p_16%2C_Penny_Cyclopaedia_with_menu.png/250px-Editing_Wikipedia_mobile_screenshot_p_16%2C_Penny_Cyclopaedia_with_menu.png)](./File:Editing_Wikipedia_mobile_screenshot_p_16,_Penny_Cyclopaedia_with_menu.png)A hamburger menu (left) opened by clicking the hamburger icon in a [Wikipedia mobile app](./List_of_Wikipedia_mobile_applications#Wikimedia_Foundation) 

The hamburger [icon](./Icon_(computing)) consists of three parallel horizontal lines, intended to resemble the lines of text in a small menu.[[2]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Gizmodo-2)[[3]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-WSJ_20130318-3) To further reduce screen real-estate consumption it may be narrowed to three vertically stacked dots ([![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Kebab-menu-ui-icon-1.svg/20px-Kebab-menu-ui-icon-1.svg.png)](./File:Kebab-menu-ui-icon-1.svg)). This has been called a **kebab**, **meatball** or **falafel button**, but still pops up a normal-looking menu.  In the [Microsoft](./Microsoft) Office 365 and Google online applications, a similar icon consisting of three rows of three squares ( [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Grid_icon.svg/20px-Grid_icon.svg.png)](./File:Grid_icon.svg) ) pops up an array of icons instead of a menu, and is referred to as a **waffle button**.[[11]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-TechR-365-11)

 

Clicking or pressing these buttons results in a vertical [menu](./Menu_(computing)) being revealed, generally the same as a one-item [menu or tab bar](./Menu_bar).[[12]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-TechCrunch_-_24_May_2014_-_Kill_The_Hamburger_Button-12)

 

## Similar characters

 

The hamburger button is not a [textual](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/text) character, and is not encoded in [Unicode](./Unicode). However, sometimes visually-similar textual characters are used by designers to create a hamburger button,[[13]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-just-13) such as the following:

 

### Hamburger button

 
- U+2261 ≡ [IDENTICAL TO](./Triple_bar)[[13]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-just-13)
- U+11054 𑁔 [BRAHMI NUMBER THREE](./Brahmi_numerals)
- U+2630 ☰ [TRIGRAM FOR HEAVEN](./☰)[[13]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-just-13)

 

### Kebab button

 
- U+1392 ᎒ ETHIOPIC TONAL MARK RIKRIK
- U+205D ⁝ TRICOLON
- U+22EE ⋮ VERTICAL ELLIPSIS
- U+2D57 ⵗ TIFINAGH LETTER TUAREG YAGH
- U+FE19 ︙ PRESENTATION FORM FOR VERTICAL HORIZONTAL ELLIPSIS

 

### Waffle button

 
- three of the kebab button characters in a row, such as ⁝⁝⁝
- U+25A6 ▦ SQUARE WITH ORTHOGONAL CROSSHATCH FILL
- U+130D1 𓃑 EGYPTIAN HIEROGLYPH D067H

 

## Reception

 

It has been argued that while the collapsed menu button is now commonplace, its functionality is not necessarily immediately obvious when first encountered;[[14]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-bbc-H-mystery-14) in particular, older users less familiar with modern iconography may find it confusing.[[15]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Loving_&_Hating_the_Hamburger_Icon-15) The location of the hamburger menu icon also depends on the specific website or app.[[16]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Hamburger_menu_icon:_should_it_be_on_the_left_or_right?-16)

 

The menu button may increase [interaction cost](./Interaction_cost) compared to a [menu bar](./Menu_bar), requiring extra clicks to retrieve the same information, albeit with the benefit of less space usage of the screen.[[17]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Tsiodoulos-17) It has also been argued that designers tend to overload these icons with too much hidden information.[[15]](./Hamburger_button#cite_note-Loving_&_Hating_the_Hamburger_Icon-15)
