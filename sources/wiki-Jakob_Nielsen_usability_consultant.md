**Jakob Nielsen** (born 5 October 1957) is a Danish [web usability](./Web_usability) consultant, [human–computer interaction](./Human–computer_interaction) researcher, and co-founder of [Nielsen Norman Group](./Nielsen_Norman_Group).[[2]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-2)[[3]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-3) He was named the “guru of Web page usability” in 1998 by [The New York Times](./The_New_York_Times) and the “king of usability” by [Internet Magazine](./Internet_Magazine).[[4]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:6-4)[[5]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-5)[[1]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-gs-1)

 

## Education and early life

 

Jakob Nielsen was born 5 October 1957 in [Copenhagen](./Copenhagen), [Denmark](./Denmark).[[6]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:0-6)[[7]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-7) He holds a [PhD](./PhD) in human–computer interaction from the [Technical University of Denmark](./Technical_University_of_Denmark).[[8]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-phd-8)[[6]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:0-6)

 

## Career and research

 

Nielsen's affiliations include [Bellcore](./Telcordia_Technologies), teaching at the [Technical University of Denmark](./Technical_University_of_Denmark), and the [IBM](./IBM) User Interface Institute at the [Thomas J. Watson Research Center](./Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center).[[9]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:3-10)[*[when?](./Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)*] From 1994 to 1998, he was a distinguished engineer [Sun Microsystems](./Sun_Microsystems).[[4]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:6-4)

 

### Website usability writing

 

Beginning in 1996, Nielsen published a [fortnightly](./Fortnightly) column about [website usability](./Web_usability) called Alertbox[[11]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-12)[*[AI-retrieved source](./Template:AI-retrieved_source/doc)*] on his now-archived personal website [useit.com](https://www.chnm.gmu.edu/digitalhistory/links/cached/chapter4/4_2_useit.htm). Alertbox was syndicated to, and eventually replaced by, the weblog and email newsletter on the Nielsen Norman Group website during the 2000s.

 

Nielsen has published several books on the subject of [web design](./Web_design).[[6]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:0-6)[[10]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:3-10) He was a Series Editor for [Morgan Kaufmann Publishers](./Morgan_Kaufmann_Publishers)' book series focused on interactive technologies.[[13]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-13)

 

Nielsen founded [UX Tigers](https://www.uxtigers.com/) in 2023 where he currently publishes articles on the topics of usability, with a heavy focus on the intersection of digital usability and artificial intelligence,[[14]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-14)[[15]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-15)[*[AI-retrieved source](./Template:AI-retrieved_source/doc)*] and publishes video content about usability.

 

### Nielsen Norman Group (NNG)

 

After his regular articles on his website about usability research attracted media attention, he co-founded usability consulting company [Nielsen Norman Group](./Nielsen_Norman_Group) (NN/g) of [Fremont, California](./Fremont,_California) in 1998 with fellow usability expert [Donald Norman](./Donald_Norman).[[6]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:0-6)[[16]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:02-16)[[17]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-17) The company's vision is to help designers and other companies move toward more human-centered products and internet interactions, as experts and pioneers in the field of [usability](./Usability).[[16]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:02-16)

 

Nielsen founded the [usability engineering](./Usability_engineering) movement for efficient and affordable improvements of user interfaces and he has invented several usability methods, including [heuristic evaluation](./Heuristic_evaluation). He holds more than a thousand United States patents,[[18]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-18)[[19]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-19) mainly on ways of improving usability for technology.

 

In the early 1990s, Nielsen popularized the principle that five test users per [usability test](./Usability_testing) session is enough, allowing numerous tests at various stages of the development process.[[20]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-useit-20) His argument is that "elaborate usability tests are a waste of resources." Once it is found that a few people are totally confused by a home page, little is gained by watching more people suffer through the same flawed design.[[20]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-useit-20)

 

### Jakob's law

 

 Users will anticipate what an experience will be like, based on their [mental models](./Mental_model) of prior experiences on websites.[[21]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-21)[[22]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:5-22) When making changes to a design of a website, try to minimize changes in order to maintain an ease of use.[[22]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:5-22)

 

### Nielsen's usability heuristics

 See also: [Heuristic evaluation](./Heuristic_evaluation) 

 Nielsen's list of ten heuristics is probably the most-used usability framework for [user interface design](./User_interface_design). An early version of the heuristics appeared in two papers by Nielsen and Rolf Molich  published in 1989–1990.[[23]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-23)[[24]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-mol-24) Nielsen published an updated set in 1994,[[25]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-25) and the final set still in use today was published in 2005:[[26]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-26)

 
1. Visibility of system status
2. Match between system and the real world
3. User control and freedom
4. Consistency and standards
5. Error prevention
6. Recognition rather than recall
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use
8. Aesthetic and minimalist design
9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors
10. Help and documentation

 

In his book *Usability Engineering* (1993), Nielsen also defined the five quality components of his "Usability Goals":[[27]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-27)

 
1. Learnability
2. Efficiency
3. Memorability
4. Errors (as in low error rate)
5. Satisfaction

 

### Windows 8 usability

 

Nielsen has been quoted in the computing and the mainstream press for his criticism of [Microsoft's](./Microsoft) [Windows 8](./Windows_8) (2012) user interface.[[28]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-28)[[29]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-29)[[30]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-30) Tom Hobbs, creative director of the design firm [Teague](./Teague_(company)), criticized what he perceived to be some of Nielsen's points on the matter, and Nielsen responded with some clarifications.[[31]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-31) The subsequent short and troubled history of Windows 8, released on 26 October 2012, seems to have confirmed Nielsen's criticism: the sales of Windows-based systems plummeted after the introduction of Windows 8;[[32]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-32) Microsoft released a new version, Windows 8.1, on 18 October 2013, to fix the numerous problems identified in Windows 8, and later released [Windows 10](./Windows_10), a complete overhaul, in July 2015.

 

### Criticisms

 

As Nielsen's newsletter and website grew, and with his use of "[acronomic](./Acronym) [platitudes](./Platitude)"[[33]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-flame-33) to describe his concepts, some critics like [Philip Greenspun](./Philip_Greenspun) argued that Nielsen's work was more about marketing himself than any particular research.[[10]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:3-10)

 

### Nielsen's usability heuristics

 

In 1990, when the Nielsen heuristic evaluation guidelines were created,[[24]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-mol-24) [user interface](./User_interface) was less complicated than it is in present-day.[[34]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-34)[[35]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:1-35) There has never been any research-based validation of Nielsen's heuristics.[[35]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:1-35) Researchers at the [University of Calgary](./University_of_Calgary) published an article in 2008, questioning if the Nielsen heuristics were an oversimplification.[[36]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-36)

 

Nielsen has been criticized by some [visual designers](./Communication_design) and [graphic designers](./Graphic_designer) for failing to balance the importance of other [user experience](./User_experience) considerations such as typography, readability, visual cues for hierarchy and importance, and eye appeal.[[37]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-37)[[38]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-38)

 

### Responsive design

 

Nielsen's 2012 guidelines, "*Repurposing vs Optimized Design"*  that web sites made for mobile devices be designed separately from their desktop-oriented counterparts has come under fire from [Webmonkey](./Webmonkey)'s Scott Gilbertson,[[39]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-39) as well as Josh Clark writing in [.net magazine](./.net_(magazine)),[[40]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-40) and [Opera](./Opera_(web_browser))'s Bruce Lawson, writing in [Smashing Magazine](./Smashing_Magazine),[[41]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-41) and other technologists and web designers who advocate [responsive web design](./Responsive_web_design).[[42]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-42)[[43]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:4-43) In an interview with *.net* magazine, Nielsen explained that he wrote his guidelines from a usability perspective, not from the viewpoint of implementation.[[44]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-44)

 

Nielsen has been accused of taking a "[puritanical](./Puritans)" approach to usability, and not being able to keep up his usability evaluations in step of technological changes.[[10]](./Jakob_Nielsen_(usability_consultant)#cite_note-:3-10)
