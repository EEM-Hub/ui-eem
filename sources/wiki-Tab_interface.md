 [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Language_tabs_on_en_wiktionary.gif/500px-Language_tabs_on_en_wiktionary.gif)](./File:Language_tabs_on_en_wiktionary.gif)Example of a tabbed interface with two sets of tabs: Horizontal tabs, at the top, allow navigation to different pages within the [Wiktionary](./Wiktionary) [website](./Website). Vertical tabs, to the left, represent languages in which a given spelling occurs, where the selected tab shows the word *jam* ('already') in [Esperanto](./Esperanto). 

In interface design, a **tab** is a [graphical user interface object](./Graphical_user_interface_object) that allows multiple [documents](./Documents) or [panels](./Panel_(computer_software)) to be contained within a single [window](./Window_(computing)), using tabs as a navigational [widget](./Graphical_widget) for switching between sets of documents.[[1]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-1) It is an interface style most commonly associated with [web browsers](./Web_browser), [web applications](./Web_application), [text editors](./Text_editor), and preference panels, with [window managers](./Window_manager) and [tiling window managers](./Tiling_window_manager).

 

Tabs are modeled after traditional card tabs inserted in paper files or card indexes (in keeping with the [desktop metaphor](./Desktop_metaphor)). They are usually graphically displayed on webpages or apps as they look on paper.

 

Tabs may appear in a horizontal bar or as a vertical list. Horizontal tabs may have multiple rows. In some cases, tabs may be reordered or organized into multiple rows through [drag and drop](./Drag_and_drop) interactions. Implementations may support opening an existing tab in a separate window or [range-selecting](./Selection_(user_interface)#Range_selection) multiple tabs for moving, closing, or separating them.[[2]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-2)

 

## History

 

The WordVision word processor[[3]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-manes19840403-3) for the [IBM PC](./IBM_PC) in 1982[[4]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-seymour19940315-4) was perhaps the first commercially available product with a tabbed interface.[[4]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-seymour19940315-4)

 [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/HyperTIESAuthoring.jpg/250px-HyperTIESAuthoring.jpg)](./File:HyperTIESAuthoring.jpg)[HyperTIES](./HyperTIES) browser and [Gosling Emacs](./Gosling_Emacs) authoring tool with [pie menus](./Pie_menu) on the [NeWS](./NeWS) window system 

[Don Hopkins](./Don_Hopkins) developed and released several versions of tabbed window frames for the [NeWS](./NeWS) window system as free software, which the window manager applied to all NeWS applications, and enabled users to drag the tabs around to any edge of the window.[[5]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-DonHopkinsPSIBER-5)

 

The [NeWS](./NeWS) version of UniPress's [Gosling Emacs](./Gosmacs) text editor was another early product with multiple tabbed windows in 1988.[[6]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-6) It was used to develop an authoring tool for [Ben Shneiderman](./Ben_Shneiderman)'s [hypermedia](./Hypermedia) browser [HyperTIES](./HyperTIES) (the NeWS workstation version of The Interactive Encyclopedia System), in 1988 at the [University of Maryland Human-Computer Interaction Lab](./University_of_Maryland_Human-Computer_Interaction_Lab).[[7]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-8) HyperTIES also supported [pie menus](./Pie_menus) for managing windows and browsing hypermedia documents with [PostScript](./PostScript) [applets](./Applet).

 

While [Boeing Calc](./Boeing_Calc) already utilized tabbed sheets (as so-called *word pads*) since at least 1987, Boeing Calc 3.0 August 1987, but possibly earlier, to be researched [[9]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-Malloy_1987-9)[[10]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-Corwyn-10) [Borland](./Borland)'s [Quattro Pro](./Quattro_Pro) popularized tabs for spreadsheets in 1992. [Microsoft Word](./Microsoft_Word) in 1993 used them to simplify submenus.[[4]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-seymour19940315-4) In 1994, [BookLink Technologies](./BookLink) featured tabbed windows in its [InternetWorks](./InternetWorks?action=edit&redlink=1) browser.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed)*] That same year, the text editor [UltraEdit](./UltraEdit) also appeared with a modern multi-row tabbed interface.  The tabbed interface approach was then followed by the [Internet Explorer shell](./Internet_Explorer_shell) [NetCaptor](./NetCaptor) in 1997. These were followed by several others like [IBrowse](./IBrowse) in 1999, and [Opera](./Opera_(web_browser)) in 2000 (with the release of version 4 - although an MDI interface was supported before then), MultiViews October 2000, which changed its name into MultiZilla on April 1st, 2001 (an [extension](./Add-on_(Mozilla)) for the [Mozilla Application Suite](./Mozilla_Application_Suite)[[11]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-11)), [Galeon](./Galeon) in early 2001, [Mozilla](./Mozilla_Foundation) 0.9.5 in October 2001, Phoenix 0.1 (now [Mozilla Firefox](./Mozilla_Firefox)) in October 2002, [Konqueror](./Konqueror) 3.1 in January 2003, and [Safari](./Safari_(web_browser)) in 2003. With the release of [Internet Explorer 7](./Internet_Explorer_7) in 2006, all major web browsers featured a tabbed interface.

 

Users quickly adopted the use of tabs in web browsing and web search. A study of tabbed browsing behavior in June 2009 found that users switched tabs in 57% of tab sessions, and 36% of users used new tabs to open [search engine](./Search_engine_(computing)) results at least once during that period.[[12]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-12)

 

Numerous additional browser tab capabilities have emerged since then. One example is visual tabbed browsing in [OmniWeb](./OmniWeb) version 5, which displays preview images of pages in a drawer to the left or right of the main browser window. Another feature is the ability to re-order tabs and to [bookmark](./Bookmark_(web)) all of the webpages opened in tab panes in a given window in a group or bookmark folder (as well as the ability to reopen all of them at the same time). [Microsoft Internet Explorer](./Internet_Explorer) marks tab families with different colours.

 

## Development

 

Tab behavior in an application is determined by the underlying [widget toolkit](./Widget_toolkit) (for example Firefox uses [GTK](./GTK)) framework. Due to lack of standardization, behavior may vary from one application to the next, which can result in usability challenges.

 

## Tab hoarding

 [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Tab_Hoarder_Bar.png/250px-Tab_Hoarder_Bar.png)](./File:Tab_Hoarder_Bar.png)The tab bar on Chromium of a browser tab hoarder. 

*Tab hoarding* is [digital hoarding](./Digital_hoarding) of web browser tabs. Users may accumulate tabs as reminders of tasks to research or complete[[13]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-y641-13) (rather than using dedicated [reminder software](./Reminder_software)). They may use multiple browser windows to organize tabs or direct [focus](./Attention);[[13]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-y641-13) however, leaving multiple windows open can exacerbate tab clutter.[[14]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-m342-14)

 

Tab hoarding can lead to [stress](./Psychological_stress) and [information overload](./Information_overload),[[14]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-m342-14) [distraction](./Distraction), and reduced computer performance. It can develop into emotional attachment to the set of open tabs,[[15]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-overcoming-15) including fear of losing them upon a [crash](./Crash_(computing)) or other [reboot](./Reboot),[[13]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-y641-13) and conversely, [relief](./Relief_(emotion)) when tabs are properly restored.[[16]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-n053-16) Tab hoarders have attributed the behavior to [anxiety](./Anxiety),[[17]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-o577-17) [fear of missing out](./Fear_of_missing_out),[[18]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-j521-18) [procrastination](./Procrastination),[[19]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-h106-19) and poor [personal information management](./Personal_information_management) practices.[[20]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-m036-20)[[15]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-overcoming-15)

 

The prevalence of tab hoarding is acknowledged by browser vendors such as [Mozilla](./Mozilla), and has inspired [memory](./Memory_management) and tab management features in browsers and [extensions](./Browser_extension).[[16]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-n053-16) Such features include tab grouping, which allows related tabs to be visually organized[[21]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-t503-21) and collapsed; conversion of tabs into a list of [hyperlinks](./Hyperlink);[[20]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-m036-20) and alternative [interface](./User_interface) paradigms, such as framing high-level [tasks](./Task_(project_management)) as first-class objects instead of tabs.[[22]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-j998-22)[[15]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-overcoming-15) A 2021 study developed [UI design](./User_interface_design) considerations which could enable better tools and changes to the code of web browsers that allow [knowledge workers](./Knowledge_worker) and other users to better manage and utilize their browser tabs.[[15]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-overcoming-15)[[23]](./Tab_(interface)#cite_note-23)[*[further explanation needed](./Wikipedia:Please_clarify)*]
